TITLE: UAE Ministry of Education AI Guidelines and Curriculum for Schools
SOURCE_URL: https://www.Entrepreneur.Com/en-ae/technology/uae-to-make-ai-official-subject-in-schools/491071

The UAE Ministry of Education (MoE) has integrated artificial intelligence as an official subject in the public-school curriculum from kindergarten through Grade 12, beginning with the 2025-2026 academic year. The initiative was approved by the UAE Cabinet and announced by Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum, Vice President and Prime Minister of the UAE and Ruler of Dubai, in May 2025. It makes the UAE one of the first countries in the world to introduce AI into school curricula at national scale.

The program was developed in partnership with Presight (a G42 company), AI71, the Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence (MBZUAI), and the Emirates College for Advanced Education. These partners support the Ministry in accelerating the adoption of technology in schools and building a future-ready education system aligned with the national vision of investing in people and sustainable development.

The AI curriculum is organized around seven key areas: foundational concepts of AI, data and algorithms, software use, ethical awareness, real-world applications, innovation and project design, and policies and community engagement. The content is tailored to each academic stage so that knowledge builds progressively.

In kindergarten, children engage with visual and interactive activities, discovering AI and digital technologies through stories and play. In Cycle 1, students compare machines to humans, develop digital thinking skills, and explore AI applications. In Cycle 2, students design and evaluate AI systems, learn about bias and algorithms, and focus on ethical AI use. In Cycle 3, students prepare for higher education and careers through advanced topics including command engineering and simulated real-world scenarios.

A defining feature of the rollout is that the new curriculum is integrated within existing school schedules without requiring additional teaching hours. AI is taught by designated teachers as part of the Computing, Creative Design, and Innovation subject. Cycles 1 and 2 receive AI sessions once every two weeks, while Cycle 3 students receive weekly instruction. The MoE provides comprehensive teaching guides, including activities, models, and lesson plans that can be adapted to various classroom settings.

Implementation is supported by nearly 1,000 trained teachers delivering the curriculum across public schools, with the school year having begun on August 25, 2025. Amna Al Saleh, Acting Assistant Undersecretary for the Curriculum and Assessment Sector, confirmed that one session within the computing subject is reserved specifically for AI education and that the teachers assigned to it have the expertise to teach AI. The Ministry planned an intensive training program during the professional development week ahead of the launch, followed by ongoing workshops and follow-up sessions throughout the academic year to ensure consistency and quality. A dedicated committee oversees the implementation process to keep delivery effective and aligned with educational goals.

The Ministry's stated goal is to give students a deep technical understanding of AI alongside awareness of its ethics, data, algorithms, applications, risks, and connection to society. Minister of Education Sarah bint Yousef Al Amiri has described the move as a strategic step that modernizes teaching tools and supports a generation of young people who understand tech ethics and can create smart, locally relevant solutions. Sheikh Abdullah bin Zayed Al Nahyan, Chairman of the Education and Human Resources Council, called it a strategic milestone in preparing future generations for a technology-driven world.

In September 2026, the UAE Cabinet extended the AI curriculum to all public and private schools and approved a wider teacher training program, moving from the initial public-school rollout toward full national coverage. The curriculum rollout sits within a broader set of education reforms for the 2025-2026 academic year, including renewed emphasis on national identity subjects and a new standardized proficiency test for grades 4 to 11 in Arabic, mathematics, and English.

For schools, the policy establishes that AI is a compulsory subject, that instruction is delivered by qualified designated teachers, that ethics is a core strand of the syllabus rather than an add-on, and that quality is monitored through Ministry oversight. Private schools following the national curriculum fall under the same requirements as they are brought into scope.
