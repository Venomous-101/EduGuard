TITLE: EU Artificial Intelligence Act - education provisions (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)
SOURCE_URL: https://casrai.org/dictionary/term/eu-ai-act-annex-iii-high-risk-use-cases

The EU Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) is the world's first comprehensive AI law. Its education provisions are a global best-practice reference for AI governance in schools.

Article 4 - AI literacy: "Providers and deployers of AI systems shall take measures to ensure, to their best extent, a sufficient level of AI literacy of their staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf, taking into account their technical knowledge, experience, education and training and the context the AI systems are to be used in." As deployers of AI systems in education, schools must ensure teachers, students, and staff have the knowledge and skills to understand not only the technical functionalities of AI systems but also how AI affects teaching methodologies, pedagogical autonomy, privacy and data protection rights, and students' opportunities. Annex III - High-risk AI systems in education and vocational training (point 3). An AI system used in education is high-risk if it performs one of four functions:
(a) Access, admission, or assignment: AI systems used to determine access or admission, or to assign natural persons, to educational and vocational training institutions at any level.
(b) Evaluating learning outcomes: including where those outcomes are then used to steer a person's subsequent learning process.
(c) Assessing appropriate level of education: determining the level of education an individual will receive or can access.
(d) Monitoring and detecting prohibited behaviour during tests: automated proctoring and exam-integrity systems.

High-risk systems face strict obligations: risk management, data governance, technical documentation, automatic logging, transparency, human oversight, accuracy and robustness testing, and incident reporting.

Prohibited practices relevant to education: emotion inference (emotion recognition) in education and work contexts is prohibited. Biometric identification for mass surveillance is prohibited. These bans apply regardless of claimed educational benefit.

Penalties for high-risk obligation failures reach up to 15 million euros or 3% of global annual turnover; up to 35 million euros or 7% for prohibited practices.
