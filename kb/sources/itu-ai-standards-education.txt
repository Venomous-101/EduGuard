TITLE: ITU AI for Good, AI Standards, and Child Online Protection
SOURCE_URL: https://aiforgood.itu.int/

The International Telecommunication Union (ITU) is the United Nations specialised agency for digital technologies. Its AI work most relevant to education and child safety runs through three strands: the AI for Good initiative, its technical standardisation programme on AI, and its Child Online Protection (COP) initiative.

AI for Good was established by ITU in 2017 and is co-convened with the Government of Switzerland in partnership with dozens of UN agencies. Its stated mission is to identify practical AI applications that accelerate progress toward the UN Sustainable Development Goals, and to formulate strategies ensuring trusted, safe, and inclusive development of AI technologies with equitable access to their benefits. The centrepiece is the annual AI for Good Global Summit in Geneva, which brings together governments, industry, academia, civil society, and the technical community. The summit programme covers national AI strategies, guardrails for safe and responsible AI, and emerging global AI governance frameworks. ITU also runs capacity-building activities around the summit, including youth engagement tracks designed to prepare young people for an AI-shaped world.

On standards, ITU develops international technical standards for AI through its Telecommunication Standardization Sector (ITU-T), including work in the Y-series on future networks and on trustworthy and responsible AI. This standardisation work is one of the building blocks cited in AI for Good's programme, connecting technical specifications with policy frameworks. For an education-focused compliance agent, ITU standards matter because they shape the baseline expectations for safe, interoperable, and explainable AI systems that schools might procure.

The ITU Child Online Protection (COP) initiative, run jointly with UNICEF and other partners, is directly relevant to child safety in schools. It produced guidelines for four audiences: children and young people, parents and educators, industry, and policymakers. The five key areas covered are: integrating children's rights into policies and management processes; developing safer and age-appropriate online environments; educating children, parents, and teachers on digital safety; promoting positive use of ICTs; and addressing child sexual abuse material.

The COP guidelines for policymakers recommend investing in digital literacy education as a core protective strategy, including teaching children critical thinking about their online behaviour, incorporating social and emotional learning into online safety education, introducing digital literacy into school curricula, and ensuring that adults working with children have the skills to support them on safety issues. The industry guidelines call for age-appropriate advertising standards, clear information to parents about services and risks, reporting mechanisms for abuse, and built-in community safety features.

ITU leadership has explicitly linked the COP work to AI, noting that children increasingly interact with AI-powered services such as online gaming platforms and connected toys, and arguing that all AI policies and regulations should take children's rights into account, informed by the COP guidelines. For EduGuard, this means ITU provides both the global AI-governance convening space (AI for Good) and the established child-protection baseline (COP) against which AI use in schools can be assessed.
