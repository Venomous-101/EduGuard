TITLE: UAE TDRA Digital Safety Initiatives and the Child Digital Safety Law
SOURCE_URL: https://tdra.gov.ae/en/aecert/initiatives

The Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority (TDRA) is the UAE federal regulator for telecommunications and digital government. Its mandate includes ICT policy and regulation, digital infrastructure, cybersecurity (including through its aeCERT computer emergency response team), and digital safety awareness. TDRA publishes digital safety and child protection initiatives through its aeCERT initiatives pages, where its child-focused work centres on raising children's awareness of their rights and responsibilities online, protecting children from cyber abuse, safeguarding children's electronic rights, and spreading a culture of awareness about electronic dangers among school students, including prevention of cyberbullying.

In practice, TDRA has run family-facing awareness programmes on children's safe use of social media and the internet, advising parents on strong passwords, app permissions, parental control applications, reviewing games and apps before children download them, and teaching children not to accept friend requests from strangers or share personal information and photos online. It has also joined campaigns with partners such as the UAE Media Council and digital platforms on family online safety, and it has issued joint statements with major platforms (including Roblox) to strengthen Arabic-language content moderation and parental safeguards for UAE users.

TDRA also sits inside the UAE's newer legislative architecture for child digital safety. In 2025, the UAE issued Federal Decree-Law No. 26 of 2025 on Child Digital Safety, a comprehensive governance framework defining the roles of federal and local authorities in protecting children and their rights in the digital ecosystem. The law establishes a Child Digital Safety Council, chaired by the Minister of Family, to coordinate policy between federal and local entities and the private sector, propose awareness campaigns, and monitor emerging digital risks. It creates a system for categorising digital platforms based on their assessed risks and impact on children, which in turn sets regulatory standards and controls for platform use by different age groups.

Key obligations reported under the decree-law include parental consent and age verification, data protection requirements (including restrictions on collecting, processing, publishing, or sharing personal data of children under 13, with exemptions for educational or health platforms under Cabinet resolution), content moderation, child-safe platform design, default privacy settings, age-rating mechanisms, restrictions on targeted advertising to children, and content-filtering systems for internet service providers. Reporting procedures for harmful content and swift action against online abuse or exploitation are also part of the framework.

Separately, in mid-2026 the UAE Cabinet issued a resolution regulating children's access to social media, setting 15 as the minimum age for creating personal social media accounts, with a tiered set of enhanced protections for users aged 15 to under 16. TDRA, together with the National Media Authority, is involved in oversight and enforcement: platforms operating in or targeting the UAE were given a 12-month transition period, and regulators can issue warnings, impose administrative penalties, or restrict platforms that fail to comply. Age verification is expected to rely on tools such as UAE PASS, official identification, and AI-based age estimation.

For EduGuard, TDRA is the key domestic compliance anchor: its awareness programmes set expectations for what schools and families should do, while the Child Digital Safety Law and the social media age rules define enforceable obligations around age verification, parental consent, data minimisation, and content filtering that any AI tool used in UAE schools must respect.
