TITLE: UNESCO Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research (2023)
SOURCE_URL: https://www.unesco.org/en/digital-education/generative-ai

UNESCO's Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research (2023) was the first global guidance of its kind. It calls on governments to regulate the use of generative AI in education, including by protecting data privacy and setting age limits for users.

Key recommendations relevant to classroom AI deployment:
- Data privacy must be protected: generative AI tools process vast amounts of user data, and education institutions must ensure student data is not used to train commercial models without consent.
- Age limits: UNESCO recommends setting appropriate age requirements for the use of generative AI tools, noting risks to younger learners.
- Human agency: AI should support, not replace, teachers and human interaction in learning. Over-reliance on AI risks reducing meaningful human interaction and student autonomy.
- Academic integrity: institutions need policies on disclosure of AI use, to preserve honesty in assessment.
- Bias and inclusion: generative AI systems can reproduce biases; educators must critically evaluate outputs, especially for diverse and multilingual classrooms.
- Teacher role: teachers need training to use generative AI pedagogically and to guide students in its critical, ethical use.

The guidance emphasizes a human-centred approach: AI in education must serve learning goals, protect learners' rights, and remain under human oversight.
