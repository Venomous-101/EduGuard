TITLE: UNICEF Guidance on AI for Children: Requirements for Child-Centred AI
SOURCE_URL: https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/policy-guidance-ai-children

UNICEF's Office of Global Insight and Policy (through its Innocenti research office) maintains policy guidance on artificial intelligence for children, developed under its AI for Children project. The guidance is grounded in the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child and is addressed to governments, the private sector, and other organisations that design, develop, deploy, or use AI systems. Its purpose is to promote children's rights in AI policies and practices and to raise awareness of how AI systems can uphold or undermine those rights.

The current version of the guidance (version 3.0, issued in late 2025) organises its recommendations into ten requirements for child-centred AI. Earlier versions framed nine requirements, but the updated version adds regulatory frameworks, oversight, and compliance as a distinct first requirement, reflecting the growth of AI legislation worldwide.

The ten requirements are: ensure regulatory frameworks, oversight and compliance for child-centred AI; ensure safety for children; protect children's data and privacy; ensure non-discrimination and fairness for children; provide transparency, explainability, and accountability for children; respect human and child rights through responsible AI practice; support children's best interests, development, and well-being; ensure inclusion of and for children; prepare and skill children for present and future developments in AI; and create an enabling environment for child-centred AI.

The safety requirement addresses protection of children from harmful content, exploitative design, and physical or psychological harm arising from AI systems. Data and privacy requirements call for minimising collection of children's data, securing it across opaque supply chains, and giving children and caregivers meaningful control. Fairness and non-discrimination requirements call for attention to bias in datasets and models that can disadvantage particular groups of children, including girls and children in low-resource contexts.

Transparency and accountability requirements call for AI systems to be explainable in ways that children, parents, and educators can understand, with clear lines of responsibility when things go wrong. The inclusion requirement covers both involving children in the design and governance of AI systems (as participants, not merely users) and ensuring AI benefits reach all children, including those with disabilities and those in under-connected regions.

The requirement to prepare and skill children covers AI literacy and readiness for an AI-shaped future, so children can use AI tools critically rather than merely consume them. The enabling-environment requirement covers the conditions that make child-centred AI possible: supportive policies, infrastructure, research, and multistakeholder cooperation.

The guidance is built around three foundational principles drawn from children's rights: protection (shielding children from harm, manipulation, and exploitation), provision (AI should demonstrably support children's learning, development, and well-being), and participation (children meaningfully included in decisions about AI that affects them, in line with their age and capacity). It also stresses that reaching a digital age of consent does not mean a child should be treated as an adult, since childhood protections do not disappear when a tool is labelled innovative or educational.

To support implementation, UNICEF provides practical tools alongside the guidance, including a policy roadmap for policymakers, a development canvas for AI teams, an AI guide for parents, an AI guide for teens, and case studies showing how the guidance has been applied in practice. A checklist of the requirements and recommendations is also available for organisations assessing their compliance.
