TITLE: Singapore's AI-in-Education Framework and National Approach
SOURCE_URL: https://github.com/isomerpages/moe-sls/blob/HEAD/_about-revised/AI%20in%20SLS/AI%20in%20Education%20Ethics%20Framework.md

Singapore's Ministry of Education (MOE) takes a framework-based approach to AI in schools, built around its Artificial Intelligence in Education (AIEd) Framework, published as the AI-in-Education Ethics Framework. The framework is intended to guide the safe and responsible development and governance of AI systems used for teaching and learning, and to keep AIEd practices aligned with the Ethos of the Teaching Profession.

The framework builds on Singapore's national Model AI Governance Framework issued by the Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA), which guides responsible AI deployment across sectors, and on the Implementation and Self-Assessment Guide for Organisations (ISAGO). The AIEd Ethics Framework adapts these general instruments to education-specific uses and risks.

At its core, the AIEd Framework is organised around four principles that shape how AI is developed and deployed in schools: agency (students remain active participants in their learning rather than relying passively on automated systems), inclusivity (tools are designed to benefit a diverse range of learners), fairness (equitable outcomes and avoidance of bias in AI-enabled systems), and safety (safeguards ensuring appropriate and responsible use). Students are placed at the centre of AI use, and adoption is expected to be responsible, calibrated to age and developmental readiness.

MOE also provides educators with practical implementation resources, including an implementation checklist for schools conducting AI trials, to make sure AI tools are deployed thoughtfully and with oversight. AI-enabled capabilities are being embedded into the Singapore Student Learning Space (SLS), the national digital learning platform, with built-in guardrails designed to minimise student overreliance on AI and to preserve teacher oversight of the learning process. Singapore also introduced age-by-age expectations: younger primary pupils are taught that AI exists inside everyday tools like maps and videos but are not set work requiring a chatbot, while from Primary 4 teachers may use MOE-built AI tools inside SLS after a safety module, and only in class.

Complementing the ethics framework is MOE's "Four Learns" model, which structures AI literacy and competency development: Learn About AI, Learn to Use AI, Learn With AI, and Learn Beyond AI. This model was adopted as the basis for two baseline AI competency frameworks developed for higher education institutions (one for autonomous universities, one for polytechnics and the Institute of Technical Education), designed to ensure a coherent learning journey from school through higher education into the workforce. From 2027, these competencies are to be infused into compulsory modules for incoming higher-education students. The national AI Council, chaired by the Prime Minister, provides strategic direction, and a Committee for Artificial Intelligence in Higher Education coordinates implementation.

For a policy-compliance agent in UAE schools, Singapore's approach is a useful comparative benchmark: it shows how a central education ministry can combine an ethics framework (agency, inclusivity, fairness, safety), age-differentiated classroom rules, teacher-facing implementation checklists, and national AI literacy progression, all anchored in a broader national AI governance regime rather than school-level ad hoc decisions.
