TITLE: UAE National AI Strategy 2031 - education and governance provisions
SOURCE_URL: https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/in-depth-research-reports/issue-brief/the-new-playbook-for-ai-leadership-the-case-of-the-united-arab-emirates/

The UAE National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence 2031 sets the goal of positioning the UAE as a global leader in AI. Education is a central pillar: the strategy calls for building a generation skilled in AI, integrating AI into the education system, and developing national talent capable of leading in an AI-driven economy.

The UAE has established dedicated AI governance institutions, including the UAE Council for Artificial Intelligence and Blockchain, the Office of Artificial Intelligence, Digital Economy and Remote Work Applications, and the Artificial Intelligence and Advanced Technology Council (AIATC) in Abu Dhabi. Abu Dhabi allocated AED 13 billion to AI development.

The UAE issued AI ethics principles and guidelines, and in 2024 published the UAE Charter for the Development and Use of AI, organized around 12 priorities for responsible AI. However, these frameworks are nonbinding: assessments note that the UAE's AI ethics frameworks lack enforcement mechanisms, limiting their practical reach. This enforcement gap is the central policy problem EduGuard UAE addresses: schools deploying AI operate under high-ambition strategy documents with no binding compliance layer beneath them.

On data protection, the UAE enacted Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on the Protection of Personal Data, the country's first federal data protection law, establishing principles for lawful processing, data subject rights, and obligations for controllers and processors.

The strategy emphasizes public-private partnership in AI adoption, including in education through collaborations between the Ministry of Education, G42/Presight, AI71, and MBZUAI. Teacher training is identified as a critical enabler: the 2025-2026 AI curriculum rollout included training for nearly 1,000 specialist teachers, with further phases planned.
