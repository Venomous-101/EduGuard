TITLE: UAE Teacher AI Training Framework for the 2025-2026 AI Curriculum Rollout
SOURCE_URL: https://www.dxbweekly.com/2025/08/uae-ai-school-curriculum-2025-education-reform.html

The UAE's national AI school curriculum is delivered by a trained specialist teacher workforce. For the 2025-2026 launch, the Ministry of Education prepared approximately 1,000 qualified teachers to teach AI as a subject in public schools from kindergarten through Grade 12. The training framework combines pre-launch intensive preparation, in-year professional development, and centralized oversight of quality.

According to the Ministry, as stated by Amna Al Saleh, Acting Assistant Undersecretary for the Curriculum and Assessment Sector, these 1,000 teachers are qualified specialists equipped with the right expertise to teach AI. Rather than asking every teacher to learn AI on the fly, the Ministry designated specific teachers to deliver the AI sessions, which are reserved as standalone lessons within the existing computing subject. Students in Cycles 1 and 2 attend AI classes once every two weeks, while Cycle 3 students receive weekly instruction.

Preparation followed a phased approach. Ahead of the school year that began on August 25, 2025, the Ministry ran an intensive training program during the professional development week for the designated AI teachers. After launch, ongoing workshops and follow-up sessions continue throughout the academic year to ensure consistency and quality in teaching. A dedicated Ministry committee oversees the implementation process, checking that the subject is delivered effectively and aligned with educational goals, including alignment with global best practices in AI education.

In September 2026, the UAE Cabinet approved an extension of the AI curriculum to all public and private schools and reviewed a far larger capability-building program: training 22,000 teachers and education professionals across the country. This second phase shifts the focus from specialist AI teachers to the general teaching workforce, with the goal of equipping educators to use AI as a support tool across lesson design, assessment of student work, curriculum analysis, and lesson planning. The program is also intended to help educators use AI to encourage innovation among students rather than treating it as a standalone technical subject. Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid framed this as a response to how quickly technology is reshaping the classroom, arguing that teachers must see themselves as lifelong learners to keep pace, and warning that outdated teaching methods would leave a generation unprepared.

Competency expectations for teachers working with AI follow a practical rather than research-oriented profile. Teachers are not expected to become machine-learning engineers. They are expected to have enough AI literacy to guide students through questions about why AI systems can hallucinate, how context and data shape outputs, how bias enters AI systems, what information must never be shared with AI tools, when AI assistance is acceptable, and when human judgment must remain in control. Ethical awareness, including privacy, data protection, transparency, and fairness, is a core strand of what teachers are expected to understand and teach. Abu Dhabi's ADEK-led AI literacy work similarly builds professional development for teachers, heads of department, and designated AI leads, embedding teacher enablement in the rollout from the beginning.

The Ministry supports this competency model with concrete resources rather than relying on training alone. It provides comprehensive guides, activities, models, and lesson plans that are adaptable to various classroom settings, so trained teachers have structured materials for every grade level.

External evidence suggests the UAE teaching workforce is receptive. [UNVERIFIED: a widely-cited 2025 survey reportedly covering ~280,000 teachers across 55 systems suggests UAE teachers lead in classroom AI adoption, but no primary source could be confirmed.] This baseline makes the training phases plausible: a receptive workforce, an intensive launch cohort of 1,000 specialists, continuous in-year development, Ministry quality oversight, and a planned scale-up to 22,000 educators as the curriculum extends to every school.

For compliance purposes, the framework establishes that AI instruction in UAE schools must be delivered by trained, designated teachers; that training covers both the technical content and the ethical and data-protection dimensions of AI; that quality is monitored by a dedicated committee; and that the program is expanding from a specialist cohort to system-wide teacher capability.
