TITLE: Council of Europe AI and Education: Framework Convention and AI Literacy Recommendation
SOURCE_URL: https://www.coe.int/en/web/portal/-/new-council-of-europe-recommendation-places-education-at-the-heart-of-response-to-ai

The Council of Europe, the 46-member European human rights organisation, has developed the most rights-based international approach to AI in education. It operates on two levels: the legally binding Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law (CETS No. 225), and a newer recommendation on AI literacy in education that gives concrete effect to the Convention's education provisions.

The Framework Convention is the first legally binding international treaty on AI. It was adopted in May 2024 and opened for signature in Vilnius in September 2024. Its purpose is to ensure that AI systems are designed, developed, deployed, and used in ways consistent with human rights, democratic principles, and the rule of law. It applies across all sectors and activities within the AI lifecycle. Its overarching obligations include legality, transparency and oversight tailored to context, accountability with effective remedies, non-discrimination including gender equality, respect for privacy and personal data protection, reliability of AI systems, and mechanisms for risk and impact management, including controlled testing environments for safe innovation. Parties are also expected to establish oversight mechanisms and to prohibit or restrict AI applications presenting unacceptable risks. Because it is a horizontal human rights instrument, it applies to AI in education just as it does to AI in healthcare, policing, or employment.

Article 20 of the Convention commits states parties to promoting digital literacy and skills. In September 2026, the Council of Europe's Committee of Ministers adopted a recommendation to the governments of its 46 member states that gives concrete effect to this article by placing education at the centre of Europe's response to AI. The recommendation calls on governments to promote AI literacy throughout the education system and society, progressively making it available to all segments of the population: pupils, students, teachers, parents, workers, and citizens of all ages.

The recommendation has several distinctive features. First, it defines AI not simply as a technology but as a sociotechnical phenomenon: the systems themselves plus the people, institutions, policies, and social practices that shape how they are designed and used. AI literacy therefore means understanding AI's effects on society, not just knowing how the tools work. Second, it frames AI literacy as a civic and democratic competence, reflecting the Council's long-standing conviction that education is one of the pillars of democracy. Third, it calls specifically for education, training, and continuous professional development on AI literacy for public officials and other relevant professionals, with particular attention to education professionals, so that teachers are equipped before they are asked to teach with or about AI.

For EduGuard, the Council of Europe's work provides the strongest rights-based reference point among national and regional frameworks: any AI system used in schools should be assessable against human rights, non-discrimination, privacy, transparency, accountability, and remedy standards, and schools should treat AI literacy as a democratic competence to be taught to students and teachers alike, rather than as a purely technical skill.
