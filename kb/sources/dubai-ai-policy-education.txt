TITLE: Dubai AI Policies and Education Initiatives
SOURCE_URL: https://www.digitaldubai.ae/docs/default-source/ai-principles-resources/ai-ethics.pdf

Dubai's approach to artificial intelligence combines city-level ethical guidelines with an economic and education strategy. The two are complementary: Digital Dubai sets the ethical framework for how AI systems should be designed and used, while the Dubai Universal Blueprint for AI (DUB.AI) drives adoption across government, business, and education.

The Dubai AI Ethics Guidelines were published by Digital Dubai (formerly Smart Dubai) as part of the Dubai AI Principles. The guidelines state the core principle that AI systems should be fair, transparent, accountable, and understandable. They are non-binding and were drafted as a collaborative, multi-stakeholder effort intended to evolve into a practical and widely adopted framework for ethical AI design and use, in Dubai and beyond.

The guidelines are organized around four sub-principles: making AI systems fair, making them accountable, making them transparent, and making them as explainable as technically possible. They provide tangible suggestions for stakeholders, and they distinguish between AI developer organizations (those that determine the purpose of, design, build, or maintain an AI system) and AI operator organizations (those that deploy or use the system). An accompanying Ethical AI Toolkit, including principles, guidelines, and a self-assessment tool, helps developers across the city ecosystem assess their platforms for responsible use. The toolkit is aimed at industry, academia, and individuals.

The Dubai Universal Blueprint for Artificial Intelligence was launched by Sheikh Hamdan bin Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum, Crown Prince of Dubai. It is an annual plan designed to improve quality of life through AI, increase economic productivity, and support the goals of the Dubai Economic Agenda (D33), which targets adding AED 100 billion from digital transformation to Dubai's economy and increasing economic productivity by 50 percent. The blueprint has four strategic pillars: the best application of AI in strategic sectors, a global regulatory and governance sandbox for AI, the highest adoption of AI in government work and services, and the best environment for AI companies and talent. It is reviewed and updated annually.

Education is built into the blueprint from the start. The initial batch of projects included appointing a Chief AI Officer in every Dubai government entity, a program to attract and enable data centers, a new commercial license for AI companies, AI and Web3 incubators, dedicated AI campuses, and an AI Week for schools. AI Week introduces students to AI concepts, ethics, new tools, and coding skills, helping align their skills with future market needs.

The Dubai AI Campus, located in the DIFC Innovation Hub, is described as the largest dedicated cluster of AI and advanced technology companies in the Middle East, Africa, and South Asia region. The Dubai AI Academy was unveiled at the Dubai AI Retreat during the inaugural Dubai AI Week at the Museum of the Future. The Academy aims to provide AI education for 10,000 emerging and experienced leaders through training and certifications delivered with world-class academic and training partners, covering how to understand AI, identify and implement AI use cases, use AI as an organizational capability, and address ethical aspects. Reports in 2026 put the Campus community at more than 400 specialized companies, with the Academy having trained more than 1,500 participants.

Dubai also hosts Dubai AI Week as a flagship annual event, bringing together thousands of business leaders, investors, and exhibitors under the blueprint and the patronage of Sheikh Hamdan. The emirate's AI governance is overseen within the federal structure by Omar Sultan Al Olama, the UAE's Minister of State for Artificial Intelligence, Digital Economy, and Remote Work Applications.

For education providers, the relevant policy signals from Dubai are: AI systems used in schools should be fair, transparent, accountable, and explainable; students should be introduced to AI ethics early through dedicated programs like AI Week; and workforce development is anchored in institutions such as the Dubai AI Campus and Academy, which provide training pathways and certifications for AI adoption and leadership.
