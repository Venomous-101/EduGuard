TITLE: UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence (2021): Values, Principles, and Education Provisions
SOURCE_URL: https://www.unesco.org/en/artificial-intelligence/recommendation-ethics

In November 2021, UNESCO's 193 Member States adopted the Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence, the first global standard-setting instrument on AI ethics. It was adopted by acclamation and is a non-binding recommendation, meaning it provides ethical guidance to Member States rather than enforceable legal obligations, but it is addressed to all AI actors, including the private sector. It deliberately avoids giving a single fixed definition of AI, describing AI systems as information-processing technologies embodying models and algorithms that can learn and perform cognitive tasks such as prediction and decision-making, and noting that ethical questions pertain to all stages of the AI system life cycle.

The Recommendation is structured around four core values, ten principles, and eleven policy action areas. The four values are: respect, protection and promotion of human rights and fundamental freedoms, and human dignity; environment and ecosystem flourishing; ensuring diversity and inclusiveness; and living in peaceful, just and interconnected societies. These values frame everything that follows.

The ten guiding principles are: proportionality and do no harm; safety and security; fairness and non-discrimination; sustainability; privacy, including data protection and governance; human oversight and determination, meaning that AI must not replace human responsibility and accountability; transparency and explainability, so that people are informed about AI decisions affecting them; responsibility and accountability across the AI life cycle; awareness and literacy, through accessible education and public engagement; and multi-stakeholder and adaptive governance and collaboration.

The eleven policy action areas translate the values and principles into operational recommendations: ethical impact assessment; ethical governance and stewardship; data policy; development and international cooperation; environment and ecosystems; gender; culture; education and research; communication and information; economy and labour; and health and social well-being. For AI policy work in schools, the education and research area is the most directly relevant, calling on states to promote AI literacy, support research, and ensure that education systems prepare learners for a world shaped by AI.

For education, the Recommendation contains several important education-relevant provisions. It emphasizes that AI in education must respect human rights, protect privacy, and avoid reinforcing bias or discrimination. It calls for ethical impact assessment of AI systems before deployment, which applies to AI tools used in schools and universities. It stresses that humans must retain oversight and final determination over AI-assisted decisions, which means educators must remain in control of high-stakes uses such as admissions, grading, or student assessment. It also calls for the promotion of AI literacy and public awareness so that learners, teachers, and the public can participate meaningfully in decisions about AI. The Recommendation further notes that AI should not be used for social scoring or mass surveillance, and its fairness and non-discrimination principles warn against systems that perpetuate historical patterns of discrimination.

UNESCO has operationalized the Recommendation through implementation tools such as readiness assessments and ethical impact assessment frameworks, and it has used it as the foundation for later guidance, including the 2023 Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research, which explicitly builds on the 2021 Recommendation. For policymakers designing school-level AI rules, the Recommendation supplies the authoritative international baseline: AI must serve human agency, protect children and learners, safeguard privacy, remain transparent and contestable, and be subject to human accountability throughout its life cycle.
