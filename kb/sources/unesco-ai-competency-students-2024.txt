TITLE: UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Students (2024)
SOURCE_URL: https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000391105

UNESCO's AI Competency Framework for Students (2024) is the first global framework of its kind, designed to guide policymakers, educators, and curriculum developers in equipping students with the skills, knowledge, and values to engage with AI effectively and safely.

The framework defines 12 competencies organized across four interrelated aspects:
1. Human-centred mindset: understanding human agency in relation to AI, human accountability, AI social responsibility, and citizenship in the AI era. Students learn that AI is human-led and must remain under meaningful human control.
2. Ethics of AI: critical reflections on AI development, safe and responsible use, and ethics by design. Covers dilemmas around AI, bias, fairness, transparency, and data privacy.
3. AI techniques and applications: foundational knowledge of AI (data, models, algorithms), understanding how AI is applied in society, and the ability to use AI tools appropriately.
4. AI system design: problem scoping, architecture design, iteration and feedback loops, and understanding the lifecycle of AI systems.

These competencies span three progression levels: Understand, Apply, and Create, reflecting increasing mastery.

Key curricular goals include: fostering understanding that AI is human-led (CG4.1.1.1); facilitating understanding of the necessity of exercising sufficient human control over AI; nurturing critical thinking on the relationship between human agency and machine agency; illustrating dilemmas around AI; teaching ethics by design; promoting respect for personal and institutional data rights; and discouraging feeding sensitive information into unsecured AI platforms.

The framework emphasizes that AI in education must enhance rather than substitute students' independent reasoning and decision-making. Students should question how AI tools arrive at answers and avoid blind acceptance of outputs. Data privacy and consent are core: learners must understand risks to privacy from AI tools processing student data.
